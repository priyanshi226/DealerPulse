# Decisions

This is **DealerPulse**, built against the take-home brief (`ASSIGNMENT.md`). An
earlier draft shipped under a placeholder name before the product had a clear enough
identity to commit to one — it's DealerPulse everywhere now (nav, tab title, empty
states, this document), no leftover naming anywhere.

## What I built, and why

The brief asks for something a CEO and branch managers would actually use, not a chart
gallery. The product is organized around three questions a manager actually asks, each
with its own tab, because "what happened," "what should I do," and "let me just ask"
are genuinely different jobs and mashing them into one page makes all three worse:

- **Actionable** — *What should I do about it?* The default landing page — the first
  thing you see is a short, ranked list of what actually needs attention, not a wall
  of KPIs. Structure, top to bottom: AI Executive Priorities (the headline cards),
  the full ranked list behind them, the deals worth acting on today, a rep workload
  view, a What-If scenario calculator, and a natural-language Ask AI box.
- **Analytics** — *What's happening?* Purely descriptive: KPIs, trends, funnel,
  conversion, pipeline, lead sources, targets, delivery performance, and two ranking
  tables (branches and reps side by side).
- **Questions** — *What do you want to know?* ~100 specific, common questions
  ("What's our conversion rate for Downtown Toyota handled by Suresh Nair?"), each
  with its own independent filters — useful when you know exactly what you want to
  ask and don't want to build a chart for it. This used to live behind a sub-toggle
  inside Analytics; it's a full top-level tab now, one click instead of two.
- **Raw data** — the row-level Data Explorer, reachable from a quieter link next to
  the three primary tabs rather than competing with them for attention — useful, but
  not one of the three questions the product is actually organized around.

This directly satisfies the brief's minimum bar: an overview (Analytics), drill-down
(company → branch → rep → deal, via the ranking tables and Actionable's View Rep/
Branch/Source/Model buttons, which open a real profile with that entity's own numbers
and flagged deals — not a silent filter change), actionable insight (the whole
Actionable page, not one insight bolted onto a chart), filtering (a global filter bar
shared by every chart on a page), and responsive layout down to tablet width.

A first-time visitor also gets a guided walkthrough (10 steps, auto-starts once,
replayable from the topbar) that actually drives the app — it switches tabs and
scrolls to the real element it's talking about at each step, rather than describing
something the visitor can't currently see.

## Key product decisions

**The "AI Executive Priorities" aren't actually generated by an LLM call.** They're
six fixed, documented threshold rules over the real filtered numbers (stagnant
deal value, a branch trailing the company average by 10+ points, mid-funnel
concentration, a rep carrying 1.6x the average load, an underperforming lead
source sized into a revenue estimate, single-model pipeline concentration). I
chose determinism over generation on purpose: the same filters always produce the
same priorities, they're auditable, and it's what actually differentiates this
from a generic dashboard — a rule-based system that *feels* smart because it's
grounded in real thresholds, not because it's unpredictable. Gemini's job is
narrower and, I think, more honest: read those same numbers and explain them in
plain language, or answer a free-form question — never invent the underlying
figures. That pipeline is literally: real data → deterministic calculation →
structured context → Gemini → explanation. If Gemini is ever unreachable, every
number on every page still works; only the Ask AI chat degrades.

**Ask AI is a full retrieval step, not a raw pass-through to Gemini.** The first
version I shipped just forwarded the user's question to Gemini, which predictably
answered "I don't have access to your CRM" — technically true, uselessly
unhelpful. The fix: the backend parses the question for a time period and intent
(a plain count, a trend question, a workload/risk question), pulls exactly the
relevant slice of real data, and hands Gemini a compact structured brief instead
of the raw question. Gemini reasons over that; it never sees the whole dataset
and never gets to guess a number that isn't in front of it.

**The Questions page's filters were shared across all ~100 cards** in an earlier
version — selecting a branch on one question silently applied it to every other
question, which is a real bug, not a nitpick. Each card now owns its own filter
state independently, and the question text itself is regenerated live from the
filters ("What is the total number of leads" → "...for Downtown Toyota handled by
Suresh Nair"), using the question's calculation metadata to pick natural phrasing
rather than hardcoding wording per question.

**The app defaulted to a fully dark theme for anyone with a dark OS preference,
with no way back to light.** That's a real defect for a business dashboard meant
to be shown to a CEO, screen-shared, or graded by someone who didn't choose that
theme — the product should look the same, deliberately, for everyone. I made light
the sole default and removed the automatic OS-based switch (the dark palette is
still defined and can be wired to an explicit toggle later, but it no longer
applies itself).

**"Rep Capacity" uses workload language on purpose, never performance or
personality language.** A rep carrying 24 active deals isn't necessarily doing
badly — they might just need help. Calling that "burned out" or "underperforming"
would be a judgment the data doesn't support; "needs workload review" is what the
data actually shows.

**Reused the same calculation layer everywhere a number appears.** Rankings,
Actionable's risk detection, and the Questions engine all call the same
`analytics/calculations/*` functions the charts use — there's exactly one
conversion-rate formula, one definition of "active," one "stale" threshold (15
days of no activity) in the whole app. New views got new *presentations*, not new
math.

## Tradeoffs

- **Client-side computation, not a real backend, for everything except Ask AI.**
  The brief explicitly says this doesn't matter for scoring. The dataset (~500
  leads) is small enough that every calculation is instant in the browser, so a
  database and API layer would have added deployment surface without making
  anything faster or more correct. Ask AI is the one thing that *needs* a server
  (to keep the Gemini key off the client), so that's the only place one exists —
  as a Vercel serverless function (`api/ask-ai.js`), not a long-running process,
  since Vercel doesn't run persistent Node servers. A local Node server
  (`server/index.js`, same logic modules) still exists for local dev convenience.
- **~100 pre-built Questions vs. a pure chat interface.** I kept both. The
  canned questions are faster for the specific thing you already know you want
  ("what's our conversion rate"), and self-document what's measurable in the
  app; Ask AI on the Actionable page handles anything that doesn't fit a
  template, or that needs reasoning across several numbers. Neither replaces
  the other.
- **The What-If Simulator is deliberately arithmetic, not another Gemini call.**
  Three levers (conversion rate, reactivating quiet deals, average deal value),
  each a simple, stated formula over the real baseline numbers — current →
  scenario → estimated revenue impact, always labeled a projection, never a
  promise. I considered routing it through Gemini for a narrated explanation,
  but the formula is simple enough to show its own work directly in the UI,
  which is more trustworthy than a model that could just as easily produce a
  plausible-sounding but different number on a re-run.
- **No export/sharing.** Genuinely useful, explicitly optional in the brief, and
  lower value than fixing the shared-filter-state bug and the dark-mode default
  turned out to be.

## What I'd build next with more time

1. **Real drill-down navigation** (branch → rep → deal as actual routes/URLs),
   rather than the current "open a profile modal" behavior — fine for the
   assessment's scope, but a real product would want shareable, bookmarkable
   drill-down state.
2. **Forecasting against monthly targets** — the data already has target units
   and revenue per branch per month; projecting current pace against the
   remaining days in a target period is a natural, deterministic extension of
   the target-achievement math that already exists.
3. **A light/dark toggle**, now that dark mode is opt-in rather than automatic —
   the palette still exists, it just needs a UI control and a persisted
   preference.
4. **Let Gemini narrate a What-If scenario** in plain language once a user picks
   one, without ever letting it touch the underlying number — a small, safe
   extension of the existing "real data → deterministic calculation → Gemini
   explains it" pipeline.

## Patterns I noticed in the data

- **Lakeside Toyota converts at ~7.6%, against a ~31% company average** — the
  single largest gap in the dataset, and the branch is small (79 leads over the
  period), so it's a real signal, not noise from a tiny sample.
- **Walk-in leads convert far better than any other source (~46%)**, while
  social media is the weakest (~14%) despite reasonable volume — the company's
  best-converting channel and its most-invested-looking channel (based on lead
  volume) don't appear to be the same one.
- **December 2025's conversion rate looks like it collapsed to ~1%** in a naive
  month-over-month read, but that's mostly a recency artifact: December's leads
  are the most recent cohort in the dataset and most haven't had time to reach
  "delivered" yet — a good reminder that a raw monthly conversion rate needs a
  cohort-aging caveat, not a straight read, and I made sure the Ask AI context
  includes enough surrounding months for Gemini to reason about that rather than
  reporting the raw number as a crisis.
- **A single model (Fortuner, in the current filter) can carry over a third of
  active pipeline value** at times — the concentration-risk rule in Actionable
  exists specifically because I saw this while building, not as a hypothetical.
